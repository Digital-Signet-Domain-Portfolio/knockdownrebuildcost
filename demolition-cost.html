House demolition cost (UK)

Budget £10,000 to £15,000 to demolish an old house in 2026, the figure both Build It and Homebuilding & Renovating publish, unless there is contamination or difficult access. Planning consultant Martin Gaine, writing in Build It, budgets £8,000 to £10,000 for a three-bedroom bungalow. Most of the cost is disposing of the waste.

Gaine: "£10,000-£15,000 is a reasonable budget for demolishing a larger house, unless there's contamination or difficult access." He adds that the main cost is disposing of the waste[Build It 2026]. Homebuilding & Renovating says the work typically takes four to eight working days. A semi-detached or terraced house costs more, because the neighbouring building has to be supported.

Asbestos and other surveys first

Demolition needs building control's agreement, and Build It notes that means environmental and asbestos surveys before work starts. On a house built before 2000 the asbestos survey is an HSE HSG264 refurbishment and demolition survey; see asbestos survey cost. Asbestos found during the survey adds to both the cost and the programme.

Section 80 notice

Building Act 1984 section 80 requires written notice to the local authority before demolition starts[Building Act 1984]. The authority then has six weeks to serve a section 81 notice setting conditions (protecting neighbouring buildings, dust control, making good). Demolishing without serving notice is an offence.

Salvage and recycling

Homebuilding & Renovating: "A contractor will list the value of any salvage items and usually offset this against the quote." Reclaimable brick, slate, timber and metals reduce the bill; ask each contractor to list what they will salvage and what they credit for it, so quotes compare like for like. Demolition carried out to make way for a new dwelling can itself be zero-rated for VAT.
