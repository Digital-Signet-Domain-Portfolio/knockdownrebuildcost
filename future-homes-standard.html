Future Homes Standard cost impact

The Future Homes Standard removes fossil-fuel heating from new homes in England and cuts their carbon emissions by at least 75% compared with 2013 standards. It comes into force on 24 March 2027. The government's final impact assessment puts the extra build cost over today's Part L 2021 at £5,160 for a detached house and £5,600 for a semi-detached house, in 2025 prices.

Oliver Wakefield-Smith · Updated 24 November 2026 · Reviewed against MHCLG Future Homes Standard final impact assessment (24 March 2026), MHCLG consultation response, Boiler Upgrade Scheme statistics, Homebuilding & Renovating

What FHS mandates

No fossil-fuel heating (so no gas boilers in new dwellings). Air-source or ground-source heat pump as the heating source. Fabric performance broadly aligned with notional dwelling U-values: walls 0.18, roof 0.13, floor 0.13, windows 1.2 W/m2K. MVHR or equivalent ventilation strategy. PV typically required to meet primary energy target[FHS].

Cost uplift over 2021 interim

MHCLG's final impact assessment puts the additional capital cost of the Future Homes Standard over Part L 2021 at £5,160 for a detached house, £5,600 for a semi-detached house and £5,690 for a mid-terrace, £4,350 on average across the expected build mix, in 2025 prices. Its unit costs put an air source heat pump with hot water cylinder at £5,460 to £6,647, against £2,803 for a gas boiler system and cylinder in a detached home[FHS impact assessment]. Those are volume-housebuilder costs for modelled homes (the detached house is 117 sqm). A one-off self build buys at retail: Homebuilding & Renovating's 2026 guide puts MVHR at £8,500 to £10,000 for a house under 150 sqm, and the median air source heat pump installed under the Boiler Upgrade Scheme in July to September 2026 cost £12,826, grant included, in existing homes.

Transition for KDR projects

The new regulations come into force on 24 March 2027. A house can still be built to the current Part L if its Building Regulations application (full plans, building notice or initial notice) reaches the building control body by 24 March 2027 and work on it starts by 24 March 2028. A KDR that misses either date builds to the Future Homes Standard.

Offsetting grants

The Boiler Upgrade Scheme pays £7,500 towards an air source heat pump in England and Wales. A self build is eligible if you built it or paid a builder to build it and it has never been owned by a business; a new build that a developer is still building is not. The self-build CIL exemption is unaffected by the Future Homes Standard.
