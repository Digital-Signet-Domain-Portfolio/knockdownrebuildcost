Knockdown rebuild vs sell and move

Selling a £1.2M house and buying another at the same price in England costs £63,750 in stamp duty on the purchase alone, at HMRC's residential rates, before the agent's fee, conveyancing on both sides and removals. None of that money buys any floor area, which is why staying and rebuilding is worth costing if the postcode is the right postcode.

Oliver Wakefield-Smith · Updated 24 November 2026 · Reviewed against HMRC SDLT residential rates, HM Land Registry price paid data

The round-trip cost

On a £1.2M sale plus a £1.2M onward purchase, stamp duty at HMRC's standard residential rates is £63,750 on the purchase: nothing on the first £125,000, 2% on the next £125,000 (£2,500), 5% on the next £675,000 (£33,750) and 10% on the last £275,000 (£27,500)[HMRC SDLT]. Add your agent's fee on the sale (a percentage of the price, so every 1% on £1.2M is £12,000), conveyancing on both sides and removals, from your own quotes. Buying up to a larger house adds more stamp duty: 10% on the price up to £1.5 million, then 12% on the rest.

End-value math vs buying up

Two numbers decide it. The value a rebuild adds is the price a comparable new house on your street would fetch, from HM Land Registry price paid data, less what your house is worth today; set that against the all-in figure from the calculator. Buying up instead costs the price difference between the two houses plus the stamp duty, fees and removals above. The rebuild holds up in postcodes people pay to stay in; it weakens where the street's sold prices cap what any house there can fetch.

Lifestyle factors

School catchment, garden orientation, commute, neighbours, established landscaping. These are the non-financial reasons most KDR owners stay. If any of them no longer fit, sell-and-move is the right call regardless of the math.
