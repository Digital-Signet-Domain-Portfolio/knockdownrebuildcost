Knockdown rebuild vs extension

An extension keeps the existing house, so for a small addition it is the smaller cheque. But it pays 20% VAT where a new replacement dwelling pays none, and it brings party-wall, retrofit insulation and structural-tie costs with it. The more new floor area you need, the more those lines count, so compare a builder's extension quote with the rebuild figure line by line.

Oliver Wakefield-Smith · Updated 24 November 2026 · Reviewed against HMRC VAT Notice 708, Homebuilding & Renovating

The £/sqm breakeven

A new replacement house averages £2,500 to £3,000/sqm in 2026 on Build It's figures[Build It 2026]. Homebuilding & Renovating's 2026 extension guide puts a single-storey extension at £2,200 to £3,300/sqm excluding VAT, and says London and the South East are likely to command a 25-35% premium. Per square metre, then, an extension is no cheaper than a new house: it wins on the size of the cheque, not the rate. Compare a builder's extension quote with the rebuild figure line by line. Then VAT lands.

The VAT inversion

Extensions are standard-rated for VAT at 20%[HMRC 708]. A new replacement dwelling is zero-rated under VAT Notice 708 section 3. At Build It's £2,500 to £3,000/sqm, 20% VAT on a 100 sqm addition is £50,000 to £60,000; on 200 sqm of new floor area it is £100,000 to £120,000. That is the line item that flips the calculation.

0% VAT. New dwelling (VAT Notice 708)

Hidden cost drivers in big extensions

Party wall surveys at £900 to £1,500 per neighbour on Homebuilding & Renovating's figures, double if the neighbour appoints their own surveyor. Retrofit insulation of the existing house to bring the building-control thermal-element test (50% rule) in line. Structural ties and steels at the junction. Roof reconstruction where the two pitches meet. A very large extension is close to building a new house around the old one, and paying VAT on it.

When the extension still wins

Listed buildings (KDR is forbidden), conservation areas with strong character (KDR rarely consents), mortgage-locked properties that cannot be vacated for 12 months, and small additions, where the VAT difference is small.
